Round-trip latency test

The same measurement for JackTrip and air.band. This page plays a click from your speakers and listens on your mic. It hears the click once straight away, then again after it has gone to the other person's computer and been sent straight back. The gap between the two is the round trip.

– ms
round trip (median). One way ≈ half.

Your side (the one measuring)

  1. Use speakers, not headphones, so the mic hears the clicks. Turn the volume up a bit.
  2. Connect to the other person with the tool you're testing (below), with your mic as its input and your speakers as its output.
  3. Press Start here and leave it for 20 seconds. Write down the number. Then test the other tool the same way.

Test 1: JackTrip

Both: install JackTrip (free) from jacktrip.org or its GitHub releases. In JackTrip's audio settings: 48 kHz, buffer 128 (or 64).

The other person (the echo side):

  1. Open JackTrip in Classic mode, choose Hub Server, and set patching to Client loopback, so whatever you send them comes straight back. Press Start.
  2. On their router, forward these ports to their computer: TCP 4464, UDP 4464, and UDP 61002–61012. JackTrip needs this; air.band doesn't.
  3. Send you their public IP address: … (this page shows each person their own).

You: JackTrip, Classic mode, Hub Client, enter their IP address, connect. Then press Start here.

Test 2: air.band

Both: use the desktop app 0.6.1 or newer (air.band), in the same room.

The other person: Connection panel (the light at the top) → tick Echo test.

You: Audio settings → Echo cancellation off. Then press Start here.

Reading it

The round trip is your input + network there + their buffer + network back + your output. It's the same for both tools, so the difference between the two numbers is the difference between the tools. One way is about half. Under 30 ms one way feels like the same room. UK↔Dubai is about 55 ms one way on the network alone.
